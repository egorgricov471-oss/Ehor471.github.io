The War began

The full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine began on February 24, 2022, around 3:40 AM, when the first columns of Russian armored vehicles crossed the state border in the Luhansk region, followed an hour later by nationwide missile strikes. That day became a point of no return, turning a localized conflict in the east into the largest war in Europe since World War II

How were the first few hours?

  • 03:40 — Beginning of the Invasion. Russian armored vehicles crossed the border in the Luhansk region, while sabotage and reconnaissance groups attacked border posts in the north and east
  • 04:30 — Putin's Address and First Strikes.

    Immediately after Putin's televised address announcing the start of the "special military operation," Russia launched massive missile strikes on military facilities, airfields, and air defenses in Kyiv, Kharkiv, Dnipro, Odesa, Mariupol, and dozens of other cities
  • 05:00 — Multi-Front Offensive. Russian troops launched a full-scale ground offensive from the north (from Belarus toward Kyiv, as well as into the Chernihiv and Sumy regions), the east (Kharkiv and Luhansk regions), and the south (from occupied Crimea toward Kherson and Melitopol)

How was it next?

From February 2022 to November 2026, the war evolved from a battle for Ukraine's survival into a high-tech duel of endurance where drones and missiles rewrote the rules of combat. In 2022, defenders shattered the myth of the "world's second army" by holding Kyiv, sinking the cruiser Moskva, liberating the north, Kharkiv region, and Kherson despite the painful loss of Mariupol, and enduring winter energy blackouts.
The year 2023 brought grueling land attrition around Bakhmut and southern minefields, yet delivered a naval breakthrough as Ukrainian sea drones forced the Russian fleet out of Crimea and unblocked Black Sea ports.
In 2024, amid aid delays and pressure in Donbas, Ukraine took the war into Russia's Kursk region while F-16s took to the skies and FPV drones became the main striking force. Throughout 2025, with Europe becoming Ukraine's primary backer amid reduced US support, long-range strikes systematically burned Russian oil refineries and airbases, limiting enemy gains to under one percent of Ukrainian territory.
Today, in late 2026, a "wall of drones" and AI interceptors have halted major tank breakthroughs, allowing Ukrainian forces to hold a strategic defense and dismantle Russia's military potential from within while diplomats seek a just peace
