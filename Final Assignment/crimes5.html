Russian Military Crimes

During the conflict, Russia has regularly committed war crimes and violated the rules of the Geneva Conventions. Here you can see some documented examples.

  1. Mass Killings of Civilians
    • Bucha Massacre and Kyiv Region Atrocities

      March 2022 — Kyiv Oblast.

      Over 400 civilians were killed. Many victims were found with bound hands and signs of torture

      Legal qualification: Violation of Article 3 of the Geneva Conventions

    • Izium Mass Graves

      September 2022 — Kharkiv Oblast.

      A mass burial site was discovered containing over 440 graves, including children

      Legal qualification: Extrajudicial executions and torture of civilians and prisoners of war

    • Siege and Destruction of Mariupol

      February–May 2022 — Donetsk Oblast.

      Approximately 90% of the city was destroyed, and tens of thousands of civilians were killed

      Legal qualification: Use of starvation and siege warfare against a civilian population.

  2. Targeted Strikes on Civilian Infrastructure
    • Mariupol Drama Theater Airstrike

      March 16, 2022 — Mariupol.

      A theater serving as a shelter for hundreds of civilians was bombed. The word "CHILDREN" was written outside the building

      Legal qualification: Attack on a civilian object

    • Kramatorsk Railway Station Missile Strike

      April 8, 2022 — Kramatorsk.

      Around 60 civilians were killed and over 100 wounded during an evacuation

      Legal qualification: Use of cluster munitions in a densely populated civilian area.

    • Attacks on Medical Facilities

      2022–2024 — Kyiv, Mariupol, Vilniansk.

      Hundreds of hospitals and maternity wards were destroyed or damaged.

      Legal qualification: Violation of the protected status of medical facilities under international law

  3. Crimes Against Children
    • Unlawful Deportation and Forcible Transfer

      2022–2026 — Occupied territories to Russia and Belarus.

      Over 19,500 Ukrainian children were confirmed deported and subjected to forced adoption

      Legal consequences: ICC arrest warrants were issued for Vladimir Putin and Maria Lvova-Belova

    • Killings, Injuries, and Militarization

      2014–2026 — All of Ukraine.

      Over 700 children were killed and nearly 2,000 injured. Children were also subjected to militarization through organizations such as Yunarmiya

      Legal qualification: Violation of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child

  4. Torture and Sexual Violence
    • Network of Torture Chambers and Filtration Camps

      2022–2026 — Kharkiv and Kherson Oblasts, among other regions.

      Over 100 detention and torture sites were uncovered, alongside thousands of enforced disappearances.

      Legal qualification: Crimes against humanity and unlawful deprivation of liberty.

    • Conflict-Related Sexual Violence (CRSV)

      2022–2026 — Occupied regions.

      Hundreds of cases of sexual violence against women, men, and children were documented by the UN

      Legal qualification: War crimes and crimes against humanity

  5. Crimes Against Prisoners of War (POWs)
    • Olenivka Prison Massacre

      July 2022 — Donetsk Oblast

      Over 50 Ukrainian prisoners of war, including Azovstal defenders, were killed in an explosion at a barracks.

      Legal qualification: Violation of the Third Geneva Convention.

    • Summary Executions and Systematic Torture

      2022–2026 — Frontline areas and Russian penal colonies.

      Over 330 extrajudicial executions upon surrender were reported. Around 90% of released POWs reported torture.

      Legal qualification: Grave breaches of international laws protecting prisoners of war.

  6. Ecocide and Nuclear Terrorism
    • Destruction of the Kakhovka Hydroelectric Dam

      June 6, 2023 — Kherson Oblast

      Flooding affected dozens of settlements, caused loss of life, and devastated southern ecosystems.

      Legal qualification: Ecocide and destruction of infrastructure containing dangerous forces.

    • Seizure of Nuclear Power Plants and Energy Grid Destruction

      2022–2026 — Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant, Chornobyl Nuclear Power Plant, and thermal power plants

      Militarization of the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant and destruction of Trypilska and Zmiivska thermal power plants

      Consequences: Nuclear disaster risks and humanitarian crises during winter.

  7. Early War Crimes (2014–2021)
    • Downing of Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17

      July 17, 2014 — Donetsk Oblast.

      A Boeing 777 was shot down by a Russian Buk missile system. All 298 people on board were killed

      Legal consequences: The District Court of The Hague issued life sentences for perpetrators.

    • Izolyatsia Secret Prison and City Shelling

      2014–2021 — Donetsk, Mariupol, and Volnovakha.

      An illegal detention and torture facility operated in Donetsk. Residential districts were also shelled

      Legal qualification: Unlawful detention, torture, and indiscriminate attacks on civilians

According to the original data, as of 2026, over 217,000 criminal proceedings concerning Russian war crimes had been opened. There is no statute of limitations for war crimes.
